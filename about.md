# Looking Back: The Path I'm Already On

| Experience | When | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| High school soccer team | Grade 10 to 12 | A |
| Working at a bakery | Age 16 | C |
| One-Armed Robber with friends | Earlier this year | B |
| Canada's Wonderland | This past summer | F |
| Building my Roblox game | This fall, still going | B |

Looking at all of these side by side, I noticed a pattern. The experiences I graded highest were the ones where I was working with other people toward something, like soccer with my team or trying to pull off a heist with my friends. The lowest ones were when things were out of my control. At the bakery I got stuck doing work I didn't sign up for, and at Wonderland I spent the whole day paying for things and never got to do what I came for.

I also didn't realize until now how much of this connects to UX. My favourite part of the bakery was trying new recipes and telling the bakers if they were good, which is basically user testing. With my Roblox game I'm on the other side of it: building something, playtesting it, and thinking about how people will actually use it. Even at the bakery, I wanted to be out front with customers instead of in the back. I think I just like being close to the people who are using the thing.

My goals for the next five years are to finish my UX degree and land an internship where I can get real experience. Looking back, I've been building toward that without really noticing. Soccer taught me how to show up consistently and work with a team. The bakery showed me what kind of work environment I don't want. And the Roblox game is the closest thing I have right now to real design work. So the path is already there. Now it's about sticking with it.
